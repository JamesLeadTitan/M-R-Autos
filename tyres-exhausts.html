How It Works

What To Expect

Worn tyres and blowing exhausts are both symptoms of something.

A tyre that has worn evenly has simply done its miles. A tyre worn on one edge, feathered across the tread or scrubbed on both shoulders is telling you something else: alignment, a tired suspension component or pressures that have been wrong for months. Fitting a new tyre without dealing with that means paying for the same tyre again next year, so we look at the cause as well as the symptom.

We will also tell you what is legal and what is sensible, which are not the same thing. The limit is 1.6mm across the central three quarters of the tread, but wet braking distances start climbing well before that, and a tyre at 2mm in November is a different proposition from one at 2mm in June. You get told where yours are and how long they have left.

On exhausts, the noise usually arrives before the failure. A blow at a joint or a corroded section can often be repaired rather than replaced if it is caught early, and where a full system is genuinely the answer we will say so and price it. Rear boxes, mid sections, brackets and mountings are all routine work here, and an exhaust fault that is failing an MOT is dealt with alongside the retest.
